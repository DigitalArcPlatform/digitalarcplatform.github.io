# First Steps

There are a few things to consider for anyone thinking about community archiving.

The first step is a community gathering to decide what your community values, what it's community-archive needs are, and who wants to participate. You may already have made some progress in your community-archiving work, so assesing what you've done and how it aligns with your goals and values is the next step.

That said, these are the first steps **we** identified. Community Archiving doesn't start or end with the DigitalArc Toolkit we built. You, and your community, will need to define, on your own terms, what is important for preserving your community history and what possibilities these processes present for your community in the future. That might mean ignoring, skipping or making major changes to any or all of this particular toolkit. We'll also link to other community-archive projects and toolkits so your community can do what makes the most sense for you.

## Step One: What are your community values? Your needs?

Building your own community archive helps your community prioritize the stories, objects, and other materials that matter most to you. This kind of archiving process values your experience, your community's participation, and your community's existing skills and abilities. The practical choices your community makes should align with these, and help fill in the gaps for, your community's strengths. A good place to begin is with these 4 activities:

- Identify 3 to 5 community values or goals that you want this archive to represent (e.g. "share our history with other people").
  - It can be hard to find a starting point for these really big questions. Consider finding a partner who can bring something they want the world to know about. Then walk through these "[Establishing Community Values](https://digitalarcplatform.github.io/documentation/docs/preEvent/assessment/communityneeds/#establishing-community-values-for-online-storytelling)" questions to help you get started.
- Ask who your community trusts to make decisions about what you want the archive to represent.
- Ask who will participate both in the short-term object collection and long-term preservation.
- Establish community standards for the presentation of content and design in the digital archive. For instance, you might need to balance choices about web site design, image size, keywords with the time and skillset your community participants have.

# Step Two: ask what your community has already done.

The DigitalArc Toolkit starts with community-organizing basics and goes all the way through building a website. Your community may not need everything in the toolkit. Find your starting point in the DigitalArc Tookit here:

- Is your community starting from scratch with no community events to use as a guide? Start with the [Planning Committee ]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/preEvent/planningCommittee/)section in First Steps and find collaborators who can help you figure out how to get started.
- Are you part of a community that holds community events but has never held a community-archiving event? Start with the [Assessment]({{site.url}}{{site.baseurl}}/docs/preEvent/assessment/) section in the [Pre-Event Planning]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/preEvent/) section and see what your community needs. Then work through the entire Pre-Event Planning process.
- Are you part of a community that already has a rich collection of history but needs guidance in taking photos and collecting oral histories? Start with the [During the Event]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/duringEvent/) section for a guide on how to collect and keep track of photos and oral histories. Then check out the [Pre-Event Planning]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/preEvent/) section to fill in the gaps for any event-planning needs that might come up as you chat about what will happen during the event.
- Are you part of a community that has already done some digitization and oral-history collection? Start with the [Post-Event Clean-Up]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/postEvent/) section for a guide on how to name and format the items in your community collection for publication on a web site.
- Do you want to publish your digital community archives on a website. That's covered in the [Publishing Your Site]({{site.baseurl}}/docs/publishSite/) section of the documentation. *If you don't want your community archive to be available for the general public, using the Post-Event Clean-Up section to organize your photos and oral histories might be enough.*
